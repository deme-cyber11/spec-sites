Executive Summary

Source 4's current phone tree is leaking roughly 40 answered calls a week — about half of every call that comes in. Callers hit the IVR, can't find the right option, hang up. Or they get transferred to a department that doesn't answer and abandon the call before reaching a person. After 5 PM and on weekends, the line is dead.

Using your own numbers — 60–80 inbound calls per week, ~50% lost, ~$5,000 in revenue per answered call — you are leaving roughly $200,000 per week / $10M+ per year of pipeline on the floor. Even if only 10% of recovered calls convert at $5K, that is $1M+ recovered annually for the cost of one Phase-1 deployment.

We propose Carolina — a custom AI voice agent built on Retell AI + ElevenLabs that:

  1. Replaces your current IVR with a single open-ended greeting ("Source 4 Industries — this is Carolina, how can I help?")
  2. Routes the call in under 8 seconds to one of 5 product specialties (Bollards, Guardrail, Lincoln, Casters, Electric Material Handling) plus Sales / Customer Service / Pallet Racking
  3. Captures the full quote intake (residential vs commercial, forklift, lift gate) and forwards to sales@source4industries.com / orders@source4industries.com
  4. Sends the caller a post-call email recap with everything captured
  5. Hooks into your existing CallRail instance so source attribution and analytics finally work end-to-end
  6. Answers calls 24/7/365 — including evenings, weekends, and the gap between 5 PM and tomorrow morning

Pricing: $999/month all-in. Replaces ~$300–350/mo CallRail spend bolt-on (which keeps tracking; we plug into it) plus the cost of your current automation stack and after-hours coverage. ROI math at the end of this doc.

2. Proposed Solution — Carolina v1 (Inbound)

2.1 The greeting (replaces the menu tree)

"Source 4 Industries — this is Carolina, how can I help?"

Single open-ended question. No "press 1 for sales." Carolina classifies intent in <8 seconds from natural-language response.

2.2 Routing logic — 5 product specialties + 3 service tracks

Caller said... Routed to Carolina's behavior
"Bollards / barriers / parking protection" Bollards specialist agent Captures size, qty, install type, application; quote → sales@
"Guardrail / Handle-It / warehouse safety rails" Guardrail specialist agent Same intake + measurement assist
"Lincoln grease / lubrication / FlowMaster" Lincoln specialist (routes to Brad) Captures fitting type, fluid, system size; warm-transfer to Brad if available, recap email if not
"Casters / wheels / chair wheels" Casters specialist Captures load capacity, wheel diameter, mount type
"Forklifts / electric pallet jacks / Apollo / material handling" Electric Material Handling specialist Captures voltage, capacity, residential vs commercial dock
"Pallet racks / shelving / installation" Pallet Racking flow Books spec call with installer team
"Where's my order / tracking / PO #" Customer Service flow Order lookup → status response, escalates exceptions
"I have a problem / damage / return / billing" Service flow Captures issue, routes to support@

2.3 Quote automation — the universal intake

For every quote-intent call, Carolina captures:

  1. Caller name + company
  2. Email + phone
  3. Product + qty
  4. Application: residential or commercial delivery location
  5. Unloading capability: "Do you have a forklift or anything to unload it on your end?"
  6. Lift gate trigger: if no forklift → automatically flag truck-with-lift-gate freight quote
  7. Ship-to ZIP + target install date

→ Fired in real time to sales@source4industries.com (and orders@source4industries.com for follow-on order calls).

2.4 Post-call email recap

Within 60 seconds of hangup, the caller receives a templated email:

Hi {{name}}, thanks for calling Source 4 Industries. Here's what we captured: {{product}}, qty {{qty}}, ship-to {{zip}}, application {{commercial_or_residential}}, unloading {{forklift_yes_no}}. Your quote will arrive from one of our reps within 4 business hours. Reply to this email or call us back at (866) 734-8848 if anything's off.

This single touch closes the "did they get my message?" loop that voicemail cannot.

2.5 Full call analytics + source attribution

Every call writes to a dashboard:

  • Transcript (searchable)
  • Intent classification + product category
  • Outcome (quote captured / order looked up / abandoned / transfer)
  • CallRail source (Google Ads, organic, direct, referral) attached to revenue when the quote closes
  • After-hours flag, hold time, total duration

This is the analytics layer Source 4 has been paying CallRail for but never fully activating. We close the loop.

8. Risk & Objections

"AI will sound fake / our customers will hate it." ElevenLabs voices in 2026 are indistinguishable from a human in routine intake calls. We'll cast a voice you approve before go-live. We're also not asking customers to "talk to an AI" — Carolina just answers, captures, routes. The interaction is shorter and clearer than the current menu tree, which customers already hate (50% drop rate is the proof).

"What if Carolina screws up and books wrong info?" Three guards: (1) every email recap is sent to the customer for confirmation — wrong info gets corrected before it hits sales, (2) we run our voice-call-judge cron weekly to score every call against quality rubrics and flag anomalies, (3) all transcripts are searchable and review-able by your team in real time.

"What if a caller insists on a human?" Carolina's #1 instruction is: if asked, transfer. We honor "I just want to talk to a person" instantly. The system is a smarter front door, not a wall.

"We already pay CallRail — isn't this redundant?" No — CallRail tracks calls, Carolina answers them. CallRail's own Voice Assist is generic; it can't run 5 product-specific intake flows or know that Lincoln calls go to Brad. We strengthen CallRail's data instead of replacing it.

"$999 feels high." Compare to one lost $5,000 quote. Or compare to hiring a part-time receptionist ($2,000-3,000/mo) who can't answer at 9 PM on a Saturday and doesn't speak fluent bollards. Or the Synthflow/Bland DIY route, which costs less in license but burns 20+ internal hours a month.

"What if the volume changes?" $999 covers up to 500 minutes/mo (well above 80 calls × ~5 min = 400 min). Above that, $0.20/extra minute, billed at month-end. No surprises.
