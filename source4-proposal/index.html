Section 1 — Current State Diagnosis

What we know from your call:

  • IVR confusion Callers don't follow all options, hang up. Conservative estimate: ~25% of calls. They don't know which extension covers their product category.
  • Transfer → no answer Once routed to a department, no one picks up. Caller abandons before reaching a person. ~25% additional loss on top of menu abandonment.
  • No after-hours coverage No coverage past 5 PM CT or on weekends. A material handling buyer comparing vendors gets your voicemail and your competitor's live answer. Industry data: 80% of voicemail callers never call back¹.
  • No source attribution You have CallRail running ($300–350/mo) but no analytics tying inbound revenue back to specific marketing channels. You're paying for tracking without closing the loop.
  • No call analytics No transcript review, no missed-intent reporting, no demand-signal data per product line.
  • Sales vs service conflict Returns, billing, and tracking calls compete with revenue calls for the same human bandwidth on the same line.
Industry context. Average IVR abandonment runs ~15% across industries; in IVR-heavy verticals it climbs to 25%+ — with consumers reporting 27% of all calls abandoned because of the IVR experience itself². Source 4's ~50% loss rate is roughly 2× the heavy-IVR average — a clear signal that the menu structure is broken, not just suboptimal.

Cost of the status quo. 80 calls/week × 50% loss × $5,000 AOV = $200K/week of pipeline never reaches a rep. Even at a conservative 5% close rate on recovered calls, that is $10K/week / $520K/year of net-new revenue Source 4 is forfeiting.

2.1 The greeting — replaces the menu tree

"Source 4 Industries — this is Carolina, how can I help?"

Single open-ended question. No "press 1 for sales." Carolina classifies intent in <8 seconds from a natural-language response.

Section 7A — Phone System Integration

Three questions we get on every demo: How does this plug in? What happens to our existing phone system? How do we keep recording calls? Answered here.

7A.1 Connecting Carolina to your existing phone system

  1. 1
    One forwarding rule — that's it We provision a Retell phone number for Carolina. You add a forwarding rule in your existing PBX or hosted phone system (RingCentral, 8x8, Vonage, or whoever hosts your line) that sends calls to that number. Your current system stays exactly as-is — no migrations, no SIP changes, no new hardware. Source 4 retains full ownership of (866) 734-8848.
  2. 2
    Staged go-live option We can run Carolina on a separate test number first — side-by-side with your current IVR — so your team can evaluate it on live traffic before cutting the main line over. Zero downside risk.
  3. 3
    When a caller asks for a human, Carolina transfers We configure a warm-transfer number (your existing main extension, or a direct sales line) that Carolina hands off to instantly on request. The forwarding chain is: (866) 734-8848 → Carolina → human extension if needed. No call ever dies in the system.

7A.2 Call recording

  • 100% capture Retell AI records every call natively — both sides — from the moment the call connects to Carolina. No opt-in, no separate recorder to configure.
  • Access Recordings are accessible in the Retell dashboard and via API export. Your team can search by date, duration, product line, or outcome. We can also route recordings to an S3 bucket or Google Drive on request.
  • Compliance Carolina answers the call — we are the answering party, so recording is legally straightforward in most US jurisdictions. For states with two-party consent laws, we can add a disclosure to the greeting ("this call may be recorded").
  • Transcripts Every recording comes with an automatic word-accurate transcript. Searchable, annotated with speaker labels (caller vs. agent), and linked to the lead data captured during the call.

7A.3 Source attribution — replaces CallRail tracking numbers

CallRail's core value is assigning a unique tracking number per marketing channel. We replicate this without the $300–350/mo subscription:

  • How it works We provision one Retell number per channel (Google Ads, organic, direct, print/radio if applicable). Each number forwards to Carolina. The number dialed is logged with every call record — so you know which channel drove the call.
  • Revenue tie-in When a quote closes, your team marks it in the CRM. We match the closed deal back to the originating call and channel. You get actual cost-per-acquisition by source — not just call counts.
  • Cost Retell phone numbers are included in the Phase 1 $999/mo. Up to 5 tracking numbers. Additional numbers available at standard Retell rates (typically $1–2/mo each).

Section 8 — Risk & Objections

Objection 1
"AI will sound fake / our customers will hate it."
ElevenLabs voices in 2026 are indistinguishable from a human in routine intake calls. We'll cast a voice you approve before go-live. We're also not asking customers to "talk to an AI" — Carolina just answers, captures, routes. The interaction is shorter and clearer than the current menu tree, which customers already hate (50% drop rate is the proof).
Objection 2
"What if Carolina screws up and books wrong info?"
Three guards: (1) every email recap is sent to the customer for confirmation — wrong info gets corrected before it hits sales; (2) we run our voice-call-judge cron weekly to score every call against quality rubrics and flag anomalies; (3) all transcripts are searchable and review-able by your team in real time.
Objection 3
"What if a caller insists on a human?"
Carolina's #1 instruction is: if asked, transfer. We honor "I just want to talk to a person" instantly. The system is a smarter front door, not a wall.
Objection 4
"We already pay CallRail — do we keep it?"
No — cancel it after go-live. Carolina replaces everything CallRail does: tracking numbers per source, call recording, transcripts, analytics, and attribution. CallRail's Voice Assist is a generic add-on that still routes to your IVR; it can't run 5 product-specific intake flows or know Lincoln calls go to Brad. Our system does all of that and answers the call. That's $300–350/mo back in your pocket, reducing the net new cost of Carolina to approximately $650/mo after cancellation.
Objection 5
"$999 feels high."
Compare to one lost $5,000 quote. Or compare to hiring a part-time receptionist ($2,000–3,000/mo) who can't answer at 9 PM on a Saturday and doesn't speak fluent bollards. Or the Synthflow/Bland DIY route, which costs less in license but burns 20+ internal hours a month.
Objection 6
"What if the volume changes?"
$999 covers up to 500 minutes/mo (well above 80 calls × ~5 min = 400 min). Above that, $0.20/extra minute, billed at month-end. No surprises.
